Traditional SEO and AI Visibility are connected, but they are not the same thing.

Traditional SEO helps people find your website in search results. It is still important. A site needs crawlable pages, useful content, sensible metadata, internal links, technical health, and a clear reason for people to visit.

AI Visibility asks a slightly different question: can answer engines understand what the business does, who it serves, why it is credible, and when it might be a relevant recommendation?

## Traditional SEO is still the foundation

AI visibility work becomes fragile if the basics are weak. If important pages cannot be crawled, if services are vague, or if the website does not explain the business clearly, answer engines have poor source material.

The sensible route is not to replace SEO. It is to extend it.

## AI Visibility adds entity clarity

Answer engines need clean signals. They need to understand the business as an entity, the services it offers, the locations it serves, the sectors it fits, and the questions it can answer.

This is where [Answer Engine Optimisation](/answer-engine-optimisation) becomes useful. It shapes the website so AI systems have a better chance of forming an accurate summary.

## The practical differences

Traditional SEO often focuses on ranking pages for search queries. AI Visibility also looks at whether tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI can describe the business accurately from public information.

That can involve:

- Structured data
- Service architecture
- FAQ coverage
- Internal links
- Public assistant knowledge
- [llms.txt](/llms-txt) and llms-full.txt
- Sitemap and robots checks
- Prompt testing and visibility reports

## No guarantees

No one can guarantee inclusion, rankings, citations, or recommendations in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI, or AI Overviews.

The useful work is to make the business easier to understand and cite.
