A visitor planning time in Oban may ask a search engine or AI assistant a practical question before opening a tourism website. They may want to know whether an attraction is open, how it connects with a ferry arrival, whether an activity needs advance booking, or which venue has a suitable event that evening.

AI visibility is partly about whether the public information available can support an accurate answer. It is not simply a count of brand mentions, and it is not a promise that a particular system will cite or recommend a business.

## The owned source still matters

Tourism information often exists in several places at once. A business may have its own website, a Google Business Profile, accommodation or activity listings, social accounts, event directories, and a third-party booking platform. Transport and destination information may sit elsewhere again.

Those platforms are useful, but they can leave important facts fragmented. The organisation's own website should provide a maintained source for the information it controls:

* what it offers and who it is for;
* its real location and operating area;
* opening, seasonal, or availability information;
* access, facilities, and suitability;
* the booking or enquiry route;
* evidence for important claims;
* a clear distinction between durable facts and changing information.

When the owned source is explicit, customers can verify an answer directly. Search and AI systems also have clearer material to interpret, although they remain free to use other sources or no source at all.

## Seasonal information needs dates and ownership

Oban tourism is affected by seasonality, events, weather, transport connections, and changing availability. A statement that was correct in spring may be incomplete by autumn.

This makes content ownership more important than content volume. Each changing fact needs a named source, a review process, and a visible date where the date helps the visitor. Events should be represented as events, with their own dates and venue relationships, rather than buried in an undated paragraph.

The same principle applies to AI visibility measurement. A prompt test is a dated observation. Results can vary by platform, model, account, location, and update cycle. It should not be reported as a permanent or universal position.

## Structured information is useful when it reflects reality

Structured data can clarify an organisation, place, event, service, or FAQ when the markup matches visible page content. It should not invent a local office, rating, partnership, or availability that the page cannot support.

The [McCaig's Tower structured visitor system case study](/insights/mccaigs-tower-structured-visitor-system-case-study) provides a relevant example. The project is an independent, unofficial visitor website. It separates event details from durable visitor information, supports multilingual content, uses explicit local relationships, and keeps deterministic answers inside approved knowledge.

No traffic, ranking, conversion, or AI citation outcome is claimed for that project. Its evidence is architectural: the information has a clearer source and a more maintainable structure.

## Direct answers should lead to fuller evidence

A short answer can help a visitor decide what to do next, but it should not remove important context. An accommodation provider might answer whether dogs are accepted, then link to the full policy. A marine operator might give a concise suitability answer, then show the safety and booking requirements. An event venue might state the time and location, then connect the visitor to the canonical event record.

This is where Answer Engine Optimisation and AI visibility overlap. AEO improves the source and its extractable answers. AI visibility observes how public systems appear to understand and represent the organisation. The two activities should be measured separately.

## A practical starting point

An Oban tourism business can begin by listing its twenty most common pre-booking and pre-visit questions. For each question, identify the current answer, its owner, where it is published, when it changes, and what evidence a visitor may need.

That exercise usually reveals whether the immediate need is clearer page content, event or venue records, improved booking handoffs, structured data, a controlled assistant, or a wider technical review.
